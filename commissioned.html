What it is

A short documentary about one person your organization actually changed. I find them, I film them, I hand you the film.

Who it's for

Companies, foundations, and nonprofits doing real good with nothing to show for it.

What it costs

$4,500 for a Test Run. $25,000 for the campaign. The Test Run applies in full toward the campaign.

What you decide

Yes or no. That is the entire list. You do not pick the person, write the brief, or choose the story.

The money is not the problem. You moved real money last year, and it did real good, and when someone at a board table asks what it added up to, the honest answer is a number that does not sound like anything.

You are short on decisions more than you are short on time. Your whole week is choosing between things. Then someone arrives to help and brings a questionnaire, a menu of packages, a moodboard to react to, and a list of names to supply. More choosing, in a helpful costume.

The proof you need already exists. It is sitting inside your own organization, in one person whose life went a different direction because of something you funded. Nobody has gone looking for them. Looking takes a kind of slow attention that nobody in your building has left.

You do not have thirty minutes to go find that person. I do. It is the only thing I do.

Why the last one didn't work

You have probably bought a video before. It was fine. Somebody came out, shot what you pointed at, cut it together, and sent you a file. You posted it once. It went on a drive. Nobody mentioned it again.

The film was fine. A file is an object. It arrives finished and then it sits there, with nothing attached to it and nowhere in particular to go, and you are the one left holding it.

So you concluded that video does not work for you. That was a fair reading of the evidence you had. One film, made one time, about whatever happened to be in front of the camera that week, and then it was over. Anyone would draw the same conclusion twice.

Mr. Curtis

There is a man in Midland named Mr. Curtis. For more than twenty years he has worked the same corner, greeting children by name on their way into school. A counselor at Fannin Elementary calls him the school's unofficial mascot.

I made a film about him. It runs two minutes and thirty-seven seconds.

Fannin posted it. Parents came up to the school with handwritten notes. Students made posters. Staff lined the car loop.

Midland ISD shared it. Fannin shared it again from their own page. Chick-fil-A Midland shared it too. The Odessa American ran a full feature. It screened once at a film festival at the Ector Theatre, and Mr. Curtis was sitting in the room when the audience applauded at the end.

Nobody planned any of that. One man, one corner, twenty years, and somebody finally pointed a camera at it.

A CBS7 anchor saw the film on Fannin Elementary's Facebook page. She came out to the celebration in May 2025 because of it, and said this on camera, standing on the corner.

"You did such a great job with it that I said, okay, we just want to do something very, very similar. Maybe not as good, but very similar. That's the vision, because it captured the joy that Mr. Curtis brings to this school every day."

Mary Kate Hamilton, anchor and reporter, CBS7 / First Alert 7 News

What the year looks like

The year is built like a television season. Each film has a job, and the jobs change as the year goes.

I go find them

I spend time in your world, talking to people, until the same name keeps coming up. You do not supply the name. Finding it is the work.

You just watch

Nothing to approve along the way. Nothing to remember. The first film premieres in month two.

The day

I shoot where the person actually is. The corner, the shop floor, the loading dock. I stay as long as they are around, in daylight.

One page

The film arrives with one page telling you where it goes and who it is for.

Then something happens that surprised me the first time. People in your building start pointing these folks out to me before I have found them. Somebody stops me in a hallway and says, you should talk to her.

Good. That is the job, and it is the reason to do this rather than a reason to wait. If you already knew who the story was, you would have made the film yourself years ago. Not knowing is the normal condition, and finding out is what you are paying for.

What if I leave before it is done?

The film belongs to the organization, not to whoever signed for it, and it outlasts both of you. It also happens to be the clearest thing you could leave behind on your way out.
